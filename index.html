last_price() reads the IEX feed (confirmed) with no timestamp check. In normal minutes the IEX print is at least one minute old 13.9% of the time (least-liquid decile 20.6%), so a freshness rule at 60/120/300 s would mark 13.9% / 5.8% / 1.4% of healthy minutes stale — every threshold is above the pre-registered 1% false-block bar. On the first IEX crossing of the trigger the SIP price disagrees 7.9% of the time, but mostly with a FRESH IEX print (36 of 449 same-minute crossings vs 0 of 4 stale ones), which no staleness rule can catch. Replayed on 235 live-rule candidates (36 engine entries, 115 sessions, 2024-09 to 2026-08) the rule changed P&L by pennies at 120/300 s (normal $+0.01 / $+0.00; per empirical outage session $+0.31 / $+0.15). False entries (SIP at or below the trigger when the bot decided): normal 1 under P0 vs 1 under P1 120 s; 20 blind stress cells 22 vs 21; 20 frozen cells 20 vs 20 — the rule removes at most one, and those it cannot see are same-minute IEX/SIP disagreements a few bp under the trigger. The 60 s variant's +$29 is one skipped losing trade, not an edge. Pre-registered bar: NO threshold passes; recommended threshold: none. The exposure outages actually create is on stops, which the rule does not touch: on open-position days an empirical outage crossed the stop line while blind in 2.3% of draws, and a 12:24-to-close outage would have missed the close-confirmed exit on 42 of 1318 position days.
